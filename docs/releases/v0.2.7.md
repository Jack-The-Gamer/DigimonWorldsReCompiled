Digimon World, Digimon World 2 and Digimon World 2003 running natively on Windows, Linux and macOS. You need your own disc image (`.bin`; a `.cue` is optional); a BIOS dump is optional.

### Download
**On 0.2.5 or later?** The launcher offers this update when it starts: click **Update** at the bottom left. **On 0.2.2 to 0.2.4?** Open **Settings → About**, click **Check now**, then **Update…**. Otherwise pick the zip for your system below, unzip it, and run `DigimonWorldsReCompiled` (Windows: `DigimonWorldsReCompiled.exe`).

| | |
|---|---|
| Windows 10/11 (64-bit) | `DigimonWorldsReCompiled-windows-x64.zip` |
| macOS, Apple Silicon | `DigimonWorldsReCompiled-macos-arm64.zip` |
| macOS, Intel | `DigimonWorldsReCompiled-macos-x64.zip` |
| Linux (64-bit) | `DigimonWorldsReCompiled-linux-x64.zip` |

The builds are not code-signed yet: Windows SmartScreen may ask you to confirm ("More info → Run anyway"), and on macOS right-click the program and choose **Open** the first time.

### Changes since 0.2.6
- **Click anywhere on a mod to open it.** On the Mods page, a mod's whole card now opens its details and options (and closes them again), not just its name. The buttons on the card still do their own thing, and clicking among the options doesn't close the card.
- **Mod lists are fresh at every start.** The launcher now refreshes the mod indexes (**Find mods**) each time it starts, so new mods and new versions show up straight away instead of up to a day later. It also refreshes them every six hours while it stays open.
- **Mod updates are checked at start-up too.** The check no longer waits for you to open the Mods page or reuses an answer from the last few hours. When mods have updates, the **Mods** tab shows how many, and each of those mods has an orange **Update to vX** button like the program's own (hover it for what's new), in **Installed** and in **Find mods**. Turn the check off under **Mods → Options**.
- Mods published from the same repository, such as everything hosted in the Re:Compiled index, are checked with one request instead of one each, which keeps well clear of GitHub's hourly limit.
